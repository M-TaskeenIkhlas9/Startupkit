10 · Intelligence & scoring

Health score, case studies, co-founder

The three interpretive engines that turn the Company Object into judgment rather than just data. None of them owns a source of truth — each reads the same object and produces advice, a number, or a matched lesson. Two are detailed here in full; the third (AI Co-Founder) has its own page and is summarised at the end.

Overview

Three engines, one object, three surfaces

Where each engine's output actually shows up in the product.

Part A · Health score

A weighted roll-up, recomputed on read

The score is not stored. Each visit, per-dimension scorers each read a slice of the Company Object, produce a 0–100 dimension score, and the overall is Σ(dimension.score × weight) mapped to a status band (strong · healthy · moderate · at-risk · critical). Weights live in config, so re-weighting or adding a dimension is a data change.

Storage note. Health adds no tables and stores no score. Weights are the only new persisted thing — a tiny config table (or a seeded constant) in the one Postgres. Recomputing on read is cheap and keeps the score honest.
Part B · Case studies

A curated library, matched to this company

Case studies are content, not company data: a library of real founder outcomes (outcome, category, story, lesson, action, workflow). A matcher ranks them by relevance to the company's current stage, category, and active workflow, then attaches the top matches wherever context calls for a lesson — the dashboard's "lessons from founders like you", each journey node's "winner move", and recommendation cards.

Fits comfortably in the one Postgres; a future upgrade path is embedding-based similarity, still inside Postgres via pgvector if we ever outgrow attribute matching.
Part C · AI Co-Founder

The third engine — already detailed

The AI Co-Founder is the interactive sibling of the other two: where health scores and case studies illustrate, the co-founder answers. It retrieves the relevant slice of the Company Object, grounds a prompt, calls a swappable LLM, and returns an answer plus suggested actions that route into workflows — and it can cite a health dimension or a case study in its reasoning. It has its own full six-way treatment.
