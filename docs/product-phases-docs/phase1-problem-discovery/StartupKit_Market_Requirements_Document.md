# StartupKit — Market Requirements Document

**Phase:** 1 — Problem Discovery  
**Version:** 1.0 Draft  
**Initial market:** United States  
**Audience:** Product, Business, Research, Marketing, Design, Engineering, and Stakeholders

---

## 1. Purpose

This Market Requirements Document defines the market need, target segments, competitive context, customer requirements, positioning, and launch assumptions for StartupKit.

The MRD focuses on why the market needs StartupKit and which customer problems should shape the product.

---

## 2. Market Definition

StartupKit operates at the intersection of:

- startup formation;
- startup legal operations;
- business banking and finance setup;
- back-office operations;
- startup software procurement;
- founder education and guidance;
- workflow automation;
- AI-assisted business execution.

The initial market is U.S.-focused startup founders, including U.S. residents and eligible international founders forming or operating U.S. companies.

StartupKit is not limited to entity formation. It addresses the broader idea-to-operating-company journey.

---

## 3. Market Context

The United States has a large and continuously renewing founder market.

Relevant market indicators include:

- The U.S. Small Business Administration reported more than 36 million small businesses in the United States in its 2025 national profiles.
- The SBA reported that more than 21 million new business applications were submitted between 2021 and 2024.
- The U.S. Census Bureau publishes monthly Business Formation Statistics, demonstrating that business creation is a continuing flow rather than a one-time market.
- Stripe states that more than 100,000 startups have used Stripe Atlas.
- The supplied startup infrastructure dataset identifies 123 tools across 44 categories in seven operating layers.

These figures should be treated as market indicators, not as a final TAM calculation. StartupKit’s practical serviceable market is narrower: founders who are willing to use a digital platform to coordinate startup creation and early operations.

---

## 4. Market Problem

The market has many strong point solutions, but founders remain responsible for connecting them.

Current alternatives commonly solve one or more of the following:

- formation;
- legal documents;
- registered-agent work;
- banking;
- bookkeeping and tax;
- equity;
- payroll;
- cloud infrastructure;
- CRM;
- internal operations.

The missing market layer is a founder-centered system that:

- determines the founder’s stage;
- creates an adaptive journey;
- coordinates work across categories;
- reuses verified company data;
- tracks provider execution;
- stores evidence;
- updates company state;
- unlocks the next outcome.

## 5. Target Market Segments

### Segment A — First-Time U.S. Startup Founders

**Characteristics**

- early idea or pre-formation;
- limited knowledge of legal and operational setup;
- typically one to three founders;
- cost-conscious;
- needs step-by-step guidance.

**Primary needs**

- know where to begin;
- validate before spending;
- form correctly;
- avoid missing legal and financial steps;
- understand what comes after formation.

**Priority:** High

---

### Segment B — Technical Founders Building SaaS or Technology Startups

**Characteristics**

- strong product-development ability;
- limited legal, finance, and operations experience;
- uses modern cloud and developer tools;
- may raise external funding.

**Primary needs**

- investor-ready formation;
- founder equity and IP setup;
- banking and accounting;
- technical stack selection;
- launch and operational readiness.

**Priority:** High

---

### Segment C — Non-Technical Founders

**Characteristics**

- understand the customer or business opportunity;
- need help with MVP, websites, technical providers, and operations;
- may rely heavily on agencies or freelancers.

**Primary needs**

- translate business idea into an MVP plan;
- choose appropriate technical setup;
- coordinate website and launch work;
- avoid overspending.

**Priority:** High

---

### Segment D — International Founders Forming in the United States

**Characteristics**

- based outside the U.S.;
- interested in U.S. banking, payments, customers, or fundraising;
- faces additional eligibility and documentation complexity.

**Primary needs**

- formation and EIN;
- banking guidance;
- address and registered-agent support;
- tax and compliance visibility;
- clear provider eligibility.

**Priority:** Medium to High, subject to legal and provider feasibility

---

### Segment E — Existing Early-Stage Companies

**Characteristics**

- already formed;
- setup may be incomplete or fragmented;
- uses multiple providers;
- needs an audit and continuation plan.

**Primary needs**

- import existing information;
- identify gaps;
- avoid repeating work;
- centralize documents and status;
- continue into launch, hiring, or growth.

**Priority:** High because it expands the market beyond new formations

---

### Segment F — Accelerators, Venture Studios, and Founder Programs

**Characteristics**

- supports multiple companies;
- values standardization and visibility;
- needs repeatable onboarding and progress tracking.

**Primary needs**

- portfolio templates;
- progress visibility;
- standardized workflows;
- founder readiness;
- controlled support and referrals.

**Priority:** Later B2B channel

## 6. Initial Beachhead Market

The recommended beachhead segment is:

> First-time and technical founders creating U.S. technology startups who need formation, legal, finance, and launch coordination.

Reasons:

- the pain is immediate and time-sensitive;
- the workflow is structured enough for repeatability;
- founders already pay multiple providers;
- they value investor-ready legal and operational setup;
- they are comfortable with digital tools and integrations;
- they provide a natural path from W0 to W1, W2, W3, W4, and W7.

Existing-company intake should still be included early because formation-only competitors may not support it well.

## 7. Customer Requirements

### 7.1 Guidance requirements

Customers need:

- stage assessment;
- clear next outcomes;
- explanation of dependencies;
- plain-language terminology;
- recommendations appropriate to company type and stage.

### 7.2 Execution requirements

Customers need:

- work completed through integrations where possible;
- human review where required;
- AI assistance for research, drafting, and extraction;
- provider status tracking;
- manual fallback when APIs are unavailable.

### 7.3 Trust requirements

Customers need:

- founder approval before sensitive actions;
- versioned documents;
- visible evidence;
- secure handling of company data;
- clear distinction between verified facts and AI suggestions;
- audit history.

### 7.4 Experience requirements

Customers need:

- step-by-step screens;
- save and resume;
- no repeated questions;
- clear waiting states;
- one recommended next action;
- visibility across the full journey without one overloaded screen.

### 7.5 Existing-company requirements

Customers need:

- document upload;
- data extraction;
- founder verification;
- gap analysis;
- completion recognition;
- continuation from the correct stage.

## 8. Market Requirements by Startup Layer

The market expects StartupKit to coordinate outcomes across seven layers.

| Layer | Market requirement |
|---|---|
| Legal & Compliance | Formation, equity, legal documents, compliance tracking |
| Finance & Banking | Banking, cards, bookkeeping, payments, financial visibility |
| Technical Infrastructure | Domain, hosting, database, auth, monitoring, security |
| Product & Development | Planning, design, project tracking, analytics, documentation |
| People & HR | Payroll, hiring, employee documents, onboarding, access |
| Customer, Sales & Marketing | CRM, support, outreach, analytics, payments, SEO |
| Operations & Communications | Workspace, messaging, scheduling, passwords, automation |

StartupKit does not need to replace all tools in these layers. It should coordinate selection, setup, execution, and status.

## 9. Market Positioning

### Positioning statement

For founders who need to turn an idea into an operating company, StartupKit is an end-to-end startup execution platform that guides and coordinates the complete journey.

Unlike formation-only services, generic checklists, or disconnected provider marketplaces, StartupKit maintains verified company state, completes work through integrations and specialists, tracks evidence, and unlocks the next stage.

### Product category

Potential category language:

- Startup Execution Platform
- Founder Operating System
- Startup Creation and Operations Platform
- Managed Startup Infrastructure Platform

“Startup Execution Platform” most clearly differentiates the product from education-only and formation-only tools.

## 12. Adoption Barriers

Potential barriers include:

- trust in AI-generated or automated work;
- concern about legal responsibility;
- reluctance to provide sensitive information;
- existing provider relationships;
- perceived ability to use free checklists;
- price sensitivity;
- integration limitations;
- variation across states and company types;
- founders preferring direct professional advice.

The product must communicate boundaries, approvals, evidence, and provider roles clearly.

## 13. Pricing Hypotheses

Pricing should be tested rather than fixed during discovery.

Possible models:

### Model A — One-Time Startup Package

Charge for a defined W0–W3 or formation package.

**Advantage:** Easy to understand  
**Risk:** Weak recurring revenue

### Model B — Subscription

Charge monthly or annually for ongoing journey, compliance, and operations.

**Advantage:** Aligns with continuous company operation  
**Risk:** Founders may cancel after formation

### Model C — Hybrid

Initial setup fee plus ongoing subscription.

**Advantage:** Captures setup value and recurring operations  
**Risk:** More complex purchase decision

### Model D — Workflow or Service Bundles

Charge for legal, finance, technical, or GTM outcomes.

**Advantage:** Matches customer needs  
**Risk:** Can recreate fragmentation

The leading hypothesis should be a hybrid model: paid setup or formation journey plus an ongoing operating subscription.

## 16. Market Validation Plan

The team should conduct:

- 20–30 founder interviews;
- separate interviews with new and existing-company founders;
- interviews with startup lawyers, accountants, and accelerator operators;
- competitor onboarding tests;
- pricing interviews;
- landing-page tests;
- concierge workflow pilots;
- provider feasibility research.

Discovery should answer:

1. Which founder segment has the highest urgency?
2. Which journey stage produces the strongest willingness to pay?
3. Which outcomes founders want managed rather than guided?
4. Which providers founders already use?
5. Which trust concerns prevent adoption?
6. Whether founders continue using the product after formation.

---

## 17. Market Assumptions

- U.S. formation remains the initial focus.
- Founders are willing to use integrated digital providers.
- Founders value a single journey and data reuse.
- Existing-company intake expands the serviceable market.
- Not all workflows need full automation in MVP.
- Formation can be the acquisition point, but recurring value must come from continued company operation.
- Professional review remains necessary for some high-risk work.
