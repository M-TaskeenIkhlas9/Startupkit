# StartupKit — Problem Statement Document

**Phase:** 1 — Problem Discovery  
**Version:** 1.0 Draft  
**Initial market:** United States  
**Audience:** Product, Research, Design, Engineering, Operations, and Stakeholders

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the founder problem StartupKit intends to solve before features and implementation decisions are finalized.

StartupKit is based on the observation that starting and operating a company requires founders to coordinate many connected activities across legal, finance, technical infrastructure, product development, people, sales and marketing, and operations.

The current market provides many useful tools, but the founder is still responsible for discovering what is required, choosing tools, sequencing work, transferring information, monitoring providers, collecting evidence, and deciding what should happen next.

---

## 2. Primary Problem Statement

Founders do not have one execution-focused system that takes them from an early idea through validation, company creation, product setup, launch, operations, and growth.

Instead, they must assemble their startup from disconnected tools, providers, documents, professional services, emails, and spreadsheets.

As a result, founders often:

- do not know what should happen first;
- do not know which steps are mandatory, optional, or dependent on another outcome;
- repeat the same company information across providers;
- lose visibility when work moves outside the product;
- fail to preserve evidence of completion;
- miss deadlines or post-formation obligations;
- complete work in the wrong order;
- use tools that do not fit their current stage;
- struggle to continue from formation into launch and operation;
- repeat work when joining with an existing company.

---

## 3. Why the Problem Matters

Starting a company is not one task. It is a connected journey involving multiple infrastructure layers:

1. Legal and compliance
2. Finance and banking
3. Technical infrastructure
4. Product and development
5. People and HR
6. Customer, sales, and marketing
7. Operations and communications

The provided infrastructure research identifies 123 tools across these seven layers. This demonstrates that founders face a broad and fragmented operating environment rather than a single formation problem.

A missing or incorrectly completed step can create downstream effects. Examples include:

- incorrect formation documents delaying fundraising;
- missing founder IP assignments creating ownership risk;
- no EIN blocking banking;
- no banking blocking payroll or payments;
- unclear customer positioning slowing product and GTM work;
- missing operational access controls creating security risk;
- poor evidence management making audits and compliance harder.

The founder therefore needs more than information. They need a system that understands company state and helps coordinate execution.

---

## 4. Current Founder Experience

A typical founder currently follows a fragmented process:

1. Search online for what to do.
2. Ask other founders, lawyers, accountants, or AI tools.
3. Choose a formation provider.
4. Enter company information.
5. Download or sign documents.
6. Open separate banking, payment, accounting, hosting, email, CRM, and HR accounts.
7. Re-enter the same information repeatedly.
8. Track progress through email and spreadsheets.
9. Store documents across drives and provider portals.
10. Manually determine what comes next.

This process has no single source of truth and no reliable end-to-end journey.

## 5. User Segments Affected

### 5.1 First-Time Founders

They often do not understand startup sequencing, legal terminology, provider differences, or dependencies.

**Main pain:** “I do not know how to turn my idea into a real company.”

### 5.2 Technical Founders

They may understand product development but lack experience in legal work, finance, compliance, hiring, and company operations.

**Main pain:** “I can build the product, but I do not know how to build the company around it.”

### 5.3 Non-Technical Founders

They may understand the market but need help with technical infrastructure, product setup, and tool selection.

**Main pain:** “I know what I want to build, but I cannot coordinate the technical and operational setup.”

### 5.4 International Founders Forming in the United States

They may face additional complexity around entity formation, EINs, banking, addresses, taxes, and provider eligibility.

**Main pain:** “I want a U.S. company, but every requirement is spread across a different service.”

### 5.5 Existing-Company Founders

They already have some completed work but lack a unified view of what exists, what is correct, and what remains.

**Main pain:** “I already started, but I do not know what is missing or what I should do next.”

## 6. Jobs to Be Done

### Functional jobs

Founders need to:

- determine their current stage;
- validate an idea before unnecessary spending;
- form the correct company structure;
- complete legal and IP work;
- set up banking, payments, and accounting;
- create product and technical infrastructure;
- establish brand and market presence;
- prepare hiring and HR systems;
- launch and acquire customers;
- track operations, deadlines, and evidence;
- understand what should happen next.

### Emotional jobs

Founders want to:

- feel confident that they are not missing a critical step;
- avoid being overwhelmed by legal and technical terminology;
- reduce fear of making an expensive mistake;
- understand who is responsible for each action;
- see visible progress toward a real company.

### Social jobs

Founders want to appear prepared and credible to:

- co-founders;
- employees;
- customers;
- banks;
- investors;
- lawyers and accountants;
- external providers.

## 7. Root Causes

### 7.1 Fragmented market

Most products specialize in one area such as formation, legal documents, banking, payroll, bookkeeping, or CRM.

### 7.2 No shared company state

Each provider maintains its own data, and updates do not automatically propagate to the rest of the startup journey.

### 7.3 Guidance and execution are separated

Educational resources explain steps, while provider tools execute individual steps. Founders must connect the two.

### 7.4 Provider-centric workflows

Existing services commonly optimize for their own capability rather than the founder’s complete journey.

### 7.5 Limited cross-workflow dependency management

Founders must manually understand relationships such as:

- formation before banking;
- EIN before payroll;
- approved positioning before GTM;
- signed IP documents before investor diligence.

### 7.6 Weak support for existing companies

Many formation products assume the company must begin inside their platform.

### 7.7 Poor long-running status visibility

Provider actions may take days or weeks, but founders often receive only emails and must manually track progress.

---

## 8. Consequences of Not Solving the Problem

Without a unified system, founders may experience:

- delayed company formation;
- incorrect or incomplete legal documents;
- duplicated provider costs;
- missed tax or compliance deadlines;
- blocked banking or payroll;
- poorly selected technical tools;
- slow launch;
- scattered company records;
- increased professional-service costs;
- reduced investor readiness;
- founder distraction from customers and product development.

The cost is not only financial. It includes lost time, decision fatigue, risk, and reduced execution speed.

---

## 9. Existing Alternatives

Founders currently combine:

- formation platforms;
- legal-document platforms;
- banks and financial tools;
- accounting and tax services;
- cloud and developer tools;
- project-management tools;
- payroll and HR platforms;
- CRM and marketing tools;
- spreadsheets, drives, and messaging platforms;
- lawyers, accountants, and consultants;
- general AI assistants.

These alternatives can solve individual problems but generally do not maintain one end-to-end founder journey across all seven infrastructure layers.

---

## 10. Desired Future State

A founder should be able to enter StartupKit at any stage and receive a personalized, execution-ready journey.

The desired experience is:

1. Founder creates a workspace.
2. StartupKit determines the current stage.
3. StartupKit identifies completed, missing, and blocked outcomes.
4. Founder sees one focused next action.
5. StartupKit completes or coordinates eligible work.
6. Integrations and providers are tracked inside the product.
7. Important actions require founder approval.
8. Evidence is stored.
9. Company state is updated.
10. The next outcome becomes available.

The founder should not need to independently coordinate the complete startup operating stack.

---

## 11. Problem Validation Questions

The discovery phase should test:

1. How do founders currently decide what to do next?
2. Which startup steps are most confusing?
3. Which errors have caused the greatest cost or delay?
4. How many tools and providers do founders use during their first year?
5. Which information is repeatedly entered?
6. Which provider processes are hardest to track?
7. Which activities require human help?
8. Do founders prefer guidance, managed execution, or both?
9. What work would founders trust StartupKit to perform?
10. Which actions must always require founder approval?
11. How do existing-company founders prove completed work?
12. What price would founders pay to reduce coordination and risk?

---

## 12. Initial Problem Hypotheses

- Founders value a visible startup journey more than another general task manager.
- The strongest value comes from completing work, not only recommending it.
- Reusing verified company information reduces friction across workflows.
- Provider status and evidence tracking reduce uncertainty.
- Existing-company intake creates a larger market than formation-only products.
- Founders will accept AI assistance for research and drafting but expect approval controls for legal, financial, and irreversible actions.
- A step-by-step interface will improve completion compared with one large dashboard or checklist.
- One end-to-end successful journey is more valuable than shallow automation across every workflow.

## 13. Evidence Available So Far

- The U.S. Census Bureau maintains monthly Business Formation Statistics, showing continuing high-volume business-application and formation activity.
- The U.S. Small Business Administration reported more than 36 million small businesses in the United States in its 2025 profiles.
- The supplied infrastructure research contains 123 tools across 44 categories and seven startup infrastructure layers.
- Current competitors generally focus on formation, legal work, finance, or back-office operations rather than the full idea-to-growth journey.

---

## 14. Discovery Success Criteria

Problem Discovery is complete when the team has:

- interviewed representative founders from each priority segment;
- confirmed the highest-cost coordination problems;
- ranked the most important founder outcomes;
- identified the first paid target segment;
- validated the need for both guidance and execution;
- selected the first vertical slice;
- identified trust and approval requirements;
- documented current alternatives;
- defined measurable problem and outcome metrics.
