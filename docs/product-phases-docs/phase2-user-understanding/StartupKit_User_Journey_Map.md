# StartupKit — User Journey Map

**Phase:** 2 — User Understanding  
**Version:** 1.0 Draft  
**Initial market:** United States  
**Primary persona:** First-time founder  
**Secondary paths:** Existing-company founder, technical founder, non-technical founder

---

## 1. Purpose

This document maps the founder’s experience across the complete StartupKit journey.

It identifies:

- user goals;
- actions;
- questions;
- emotions;
- pain points;
- StartupKit responses;
- core system usage;
- success signals.

The journey is designed to be adaptive. Not every founder completes every step in the same order.

---

## 2. Journey Summary

The primary founder journey is:

> Discover → Validate → Plan → Establish → Build → Launch → Operate → Grow

StartupKit should show the full direction at a high level while presenting only the current step in the working interface.

---

## 3. High-Level Journey Map

| Stage | Founder Goal | Main Founder Actions | StartupKit Role | Typical Emotion | Success Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | Understand the problem | Describe idea, customer, and pain | Guide discovery and research | Curious, uncertain | Clear problem statement |
| Validate | Confirm demand | Interviews, tests, evidence | Structure validation and summarize findings | Doubtful, hopeful | Go, revise, or stop decision |
| Plan | Define solution and MVP | Choose scope, model, priorities | Create MVP and product plan | Focused, cautious | Approved MVP plan |
| Establish | Create the company foundation | Provide founder and company details | Coordinate formation, legal, and finance | Anxious, committed | Formed and documented company |
| Build | Create product and operating setup | Review technical, brand, and tool choices | Coordinate setup and integrations | Busy, excited | Working product foundation |
| Launch | Reach customers | Approve messaging, connect CRM/payments | Prepare and track launch readiness | Nervous, motivated | Public launch and initial users |
| Operate | Run the company | Complete finance, HR, compliance, support | Track recurring work and deadlines | Overloaded, responsible | Stable operations |
| Grow | Improve and scale | Hire, measure, fundraise, expand | Coordinate growth-readiness workflows | Ambitious, pressured | Repeatable growth readiness |

# 4. Detailed Journey — First-Time Founder

## Stage 0 — Awareness and Entry

### Founder Goal

Find a trustworthy way to turn an idea into a real startup.

### Founder Actions

- searches online;
- compares formation services and startup checklists;
- asks friends or founders;
- visits StartupKit;
- reads the product explanation;
- starts the assessment.

### Founder Questions

- Is this only a formation service?
- Will StartupKit actually complete work?
- Can I start before I have a company?
- How much will it cost?
- Can I trust it with sensitive information?

### Pain Points

- too many disconnected services;
- unclear differences between tools;
- concern that the product is only a checklist;
- fear of legal or financial mistakes.

### StartupKit Response

- explain the idea-to-growth journey;
- explain guidance plus execution;
- show supported provider categories;
- explain founder approvals;
- provide a free or low-friction stage assessment.

### Core Used

- authentication;
- workspace creation;
- tenancy;
- onboarding state;
- analytics.

### Success Signal

Founder creates a workspace and begins W0.

---

## Stage 1 — Workspace and Starting Path

### Founder Goal

Create a secure place for the startup journey and begin from the correct point.

### Founder Actions

- registers;
- creates workspace;
- chooses new-company or existing-company path;
- invites co-founder if needed.

### Founder Questions

- What is a workspace?
- Can I add a co-founder later?
- What if I already formed the company?
- Will my progress be saved?

### Pain Points

- long signup process;
- asking for too much information too early;
- unclear role permissions.

### StartupKit Response

- minimal account creation;
- automatic workspace setup;
- clear starting-path choice;
- save and resume;
- explain role and privacy controls.

### Core Used

- tenancy;
- user and membership management;
- Company Object;
- audit;
- security.

### Success Signal

Workspace is active and correct W0 route starts.

---

## Stage 2 — Stage Assessment

### Founder Goal

Understand the current startup stage and what should happen next.

### Founder Actions

- answers focused questions;
- describes idea, validation, product, company, and current setup;
- reviews the recommended route;
- confirms or changes the route.

### Founder Questions

- Why are you asking this?
- Am I ready to form?
- Should I validate first?
- What happens if I choose the wrong route?

### Pain Points

- long forms;
- unfamiliar terminology;
- recommendations without explanation;
- inability to change answers.

### StartupKit Response

- step-by-step questions;
- progressive disclosure;
- plain-language explanations;
- recommendation rationale;
- founder confirmation and override.

### Core Used

- workflow orchestration;
- tasks;
- Company Object;
- rules;
- approvals;
- read models.

### Success Signal

Founder confirms a route and receives the first journey plan.

---

## Stage 3 — Discovery and Validation

### Founder Goal

Determine whether the problem is important enough to pursue.

### Founder Actions

- define target customer;
- document the problem;
- prepare interview questions;
- conduct interviews;
- record findings;
- review validation summary.

### Founder Questions

- Who should I interview?
- How many interviews are enough?
- What evidence shows demand?
- Should I continue, revise, or stop?

### Pain Points

- confirmation bias;
- vague customer definition;
- building too early;
- scattered research notes.

### StartupKit Response

- create discovery plan;
- generate interview structure;
- store evidence;
- summarize recurring themes;
- identify assumptions;
- support go, revise, or stop decision.

### Core Used

- W0 workflow;
- AI research and summarization;
- tasks;
- documents/evidence;
- Company Object.

### Success Signal

Founder confirms problem validation status and next decision.

### Emotional State

Uncertain → informed → confident or redirected.

---

## Stage 4 — MVP Planning

### Founder Goal

Define the smallest useful product or service to test the solution.

### Founder Actions

- define solution;
- select core use case;
- choose MVP scope;
- identify technical or no-code path;
- approve initial requirements and roadmap.

### Founder Questions

- What should the MVP include?
- What can wait?
- Do I need a technical co-founder?
- How much should I build before launch?

### Pain Points

- oversized MVP;
- unclear priorities;
- conflicting technical advice;
- no connection between validation and product scope.

### StartupKit Response

- connect validation findings to requirements;
- prioritize must-have capabilities;
- suggest an appropriate implementation path;
- produce MVP brief and initial roadmap;
- identify formation readiness.

### Core Used

- W0/W4 workflow;
- AI assistance;
- product configuration;
- tasks;
- approvals;
- documents.

### Success Signal

Founder approves an MVP plan and next-stage route.

---

## Stage 5 — Formation and Company Establishment

### Founder Goal

Create the legal company correctly and preserve ownership records.

### Founder Actions

- choose entity and jurisdiction;
- enter founder details;
- review ownership and vesting;
- review formation package;
- approve filing;
- complete identity checks;
- sign documents;
- wait for provider completion.

### Founder Questions

- LLC or C-Corp?
- Delaware or my home state?
- What is vesting?
- What am I approving?
- How long will filing take?
- What must happen after formation?

### Pain Points

- legal terminology;
- repeated data entry;
- provider waiting periods;
- hidden post-formation obligations;
- uncertainty about document versions.

### StartupKit Response

- explain choices;
- reuse verified facts;
- generate or prepare documents;
- request exact-version approval;
- submit through provider integration;
- show waiting states;
- store evidence;
- update Company Object;
- unlock post-formation work.

### Core Used

- W1;
- Company Object;
- documents;
- approvals;
- integration runtime;
- provider adapter;
- evidence;
- events;
- notifications.

### Success Signal

Entity is formed, required evidence is stored, and next workflows unlock.

### Emotional State

Anxious → committed → relieved.

---

## Stage 6 — Legal and Financial Foundation

### Founder Goal

Protect the company and make it financially operational.

### Founder Actions

- review founder and IP documents;
- sign agreements;
- complete EIN steps;
- submit bank application;
- complete KYC;
- connect payments and accounting;
- review deadlines.

### Founder Questions

- Which documents are mandatory?
- Is the IP owned by the company?
- Why is the bank requesting more information?
- When can I accept payments?
- What deadlines must I track?

### Pain Points

- work spread across providers;
- legal review delays;
- bank rejection or KYC requests;
- unclear completion proof;
- repeated founder information.

### StartupKit Response

- identify required documents;
- coordinate drafting and review;
- track signatures;
- track provider status;
- create missing-information tasks;
- store evidence;
- update W2 and W3 status;
- calculate deadlines.

### Core Used

- W2 and W3;
- documents;
- approvals;
- integration runtime;
- compliance;
- tasks;
- evidence;
- Company Object.

### Success Signal

Required legal documents are signed and financial setup reaches the target state.

---

## Stage 7 — Product, Technical, and Brand Setup

### Founder Goal

Create the product foundation and a credible public presence.

### Founder Actions

- select website or software-product path;
- choose domain;
- approve technical recommendations;
- connect source control, hosting, email, analytics;
- define customer profile and positioning;
- review brand and website output.

### Founder Questions

- Do I need custom software?
- Which tools are necessary now?
- How do I know setup is complete?
- Is the website ready for customers?
- Does the messaging match the customer?

### Pain Points

- over-engineering;
- fragmented technical tools;
- agency dependence;
- unclear ownership and access;
- brand and product work becoming disconnected.

### StartupKit Response

- recommend stage-appropriate setup;
- connect provider integrations;
- track configuration status;
- preserve ownership and access records;
- connect ICP and positioning to website output;
- require review before publication.

### Core Used

- W4, W5, and W8;
- integrations;
- work items;
- approvals;
- evidence;
- Company Object;
- read models.

### Success Signal

Required technical, brand, website, and operational outcomes are verified.

---

## Stage 8 — Launch and Customer Acquisition

### Founder Goal

Reach customers and begin learning from real usage.

### Founder Actions

- approve launch messaging;
- connect CRM;
- configure payments and support;
- prepare outreach;
- launch website or product;
- track responses and initial customers.

### Founder Questions

- Are we ready to launch?
- Who should we contact first?
- How do we track leads and feedback?
- Can customers pay and receive support?
- Which metrics matter now?

### Pain Points

- launch checklist overload;
- missing payment or analytics setup;
- unclear sales process;
- inconsistent messaging;
- no feedback loop.

### StartupKit Response

- calculate launch readiness;
- identify blockers;
- coordinate CRM, analytics, payments, and support;
- generate or assist with outreach;
- track launch evidence;
- create post-launch tasks.

### Core Used

- W7;
- integrations;
- tasks;
- AI assistance;
- evidence;
- Company Object;
- dashboard projections.

### Success Signal

Company launches and records initial customer activity or revenue.

### Emotional State

Nervous → energized → learning-focused.

---

## Stage 9 — Operations and Hiring

### Founder Goal

Run the company reliably and add team members safely.

### Founder Actions

- complete recurring finance and compliance work;
- invite team members;
- set up payroll;
- issue employment documents;
- configure tools and permissions;
- monitor deadlines and support activity.

### Founder Questions

- Are we ready to hire?
- Which documents are required?
- Is payroll active?
- Who has access to company systems?
- What recurring obligations are coming?

### Pain Points

- operational work interrupts product and sales;
- unclear ownership;
- missed deadlines;
- access-control risk;
- scattered HR and finance data.

### StartupKit Response

- track hiring readiness;
- coordinate payroll and HR setup;
- manage employment documents;
- track access and tools;
- calculate recurring deadlines;
- show one prioritized work queue.

### Core Used

- W3, W6, W8;
- compliance;
- documents;
- integrations;
- tasks;
- notifications;
- security.

### Success Signal

Company can hire and operate with required records, tools, and controls.

---

## Stage 10 — Growth and Fundraising Readiness

### Founder Goal

Prepare the company to scale, hire, or raise funding.

### Founder Actions

- review company records;
- correct missing documents;
- track metrics;
- prepare investor materials;
- build a data room;
- plan hiring and infrastructure changes.

### Founder Questions

- Are we investor-ready?
- Are the cap table and legal documents complete?
- Which metrics should we present?
- What gaps could delay diligence?
- What should we improve before raising?

### Pain Points

- missing historical evidence;
- inconsistent company facts;
- unorganized documents;
- weak metrics;
- last-minute legal cleanup.

### StartupKit Response

- assess readiness;
- identify missing evidence;
- organize documents;
- surface legal, finance, technical, and operational gaps;
- create prioritized remediation work;
- support future fundraising workflow.

### Core Used

- Company Object;
- evidence;
- documents;
- compliance;
- read models;
- future fundraising workflow.

### Success Signal

Company has a clear growth or fundraising-readiness status and remediation plan.

# 5. Existing-Company Alternative Journey

| Step | Founder Action | StartupKit Response | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enter | Select “I already have a company” | Starts existing-company route | Correct onboarding |
| Import | Enter details and upload documents | Stores files and extracts candidate facts | Initial company profile |
| Verify | Approve or correct extracted facts | Updates Company Object | Verified state |
| Assess | Connect providers and answer gap questions | Evaluates W1–W8 | Gap analysis |
| Prioritize | Review missing and risky items | Creates corrective journey | Personalized plan |
| Execute | Complete tasks and integrations | Tracks provider/manual work | Gaps resolved |
| Continue | Select next company goal | Activates relevant workflows | Ongoing journey |

# 7. Key Moments That Matter

1. First route recommendation
2. First completed outcome
3. First document approval
4. First provider submission
5. First asynchronous provider completion
6. Company formation confirmed
7. Bank or payment activation
8. Website or product goes live
9. First customer or revenue event
10. First recurring deadline completed
11. First employee onboarded
12. Growth or fundraising readiness confirmed

These moments should be clearly acknowledged in the product.

---

# 8. Journey-Wide Requirements

The experience must:

- show one focused next action;
- preserve a high-level journey view;
- save and resume;
- reuse verified information;
- distinguish founder, provider, platform, and specialist responsibility;
- explain waiting states;
- support manual fallback;
- preserve evidence;
- update Company Object only after verification;
- unlock dependent workflows;
- support re-entry when company circumstances change.

---

# 9. Journey Metrics

The team should measure:

- workspace-to-assessment conversion;
- W0 completion;
- time to first completed outcome;
- stage-to-stage progression;
- founder-blocked time;
- provider waiting time;
- task abandonment;
- document approval time;
- workflow completion;
- launch-readiness completion;
- recurring usage after formation;
- founder satisfaction at each stage.

---

# 10. Research and Validation Plan

The journey map should be validated through:

- founder interviews;
- usability tests;
- competitor walkthroughs;
- concierge pilots;
- provider-status tests;
- existing-company audit pilots;
- diary studies during formation and launch.

Validation should identify:

- missing stages;
- unnecessary steps;
- emotional drop-off points;
- confusing terminology;
- tasks founders will not trust to automate;
- points where human support is required;
- reasons founders abandon the journey.
