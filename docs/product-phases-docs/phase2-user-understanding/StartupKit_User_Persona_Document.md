# StartupKit — User Persona Document

**Phase:** 2 — User Understanding  
**Version:** 1.0 Draft  
**Initial market:** United States  
**Audience:** Product, Design, Engineering, Research, Operations, and Stakeholders

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the primary users of StartupKit, their goals, frustrations, behaviors, trust needs, and product expectations.

The personas are intended to guide:

- product priorities;
- onboarding design;
- workflow design;
- user stories;
- interface decisions;
- communication style;
- support requirements;
- integration priorities.

These personas are working hypotheses and should be validated through founder interviews and product testing.

---

## 2. Persona Summary

| Persona | Primary Goal | Main Problem | Product Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-Time Founder | Turn an idea into a real company | Does not know the correct steps or order | Primary |
| Technical Founder | Build the company around the product | Strong technically, weaker in legal and operations | Primary |
| Non-Technical Founder | Turn market knowledge into a launched product | Needs technical and execution support | Primary |
| Existing-Company Founder | Fix gaps and continue from the correct stage | Work is fragmented and incomplete | Primary |
| International Founder | Create and operate a U.S. company | Faces extra provider and eligibility complexity | Secondary |
| Co-Founder | Participate in decisions and ownership | Needs secure, limited participation | Supporting |
| Specialist/Admin | Review or complete assigned work | Needs context, permissions, and traceability | Supporting |

# 3. Primary Persona — First-Time Founder

## Profile

**Name:** Aisha  
**Age range:** 22–35  
**Role:** Aspiring or first-time founder  
**Startup stage:** Idea, early validation, or pre-formation  
**Team size:** Usually one to three founders  
**Technical knowledge:** Varies  
**Business experience:** Limited  
**Budget:** Cost-conscious  
**Decision style:** Research-heavy but uncertain

## Background

Aisha has identified a problem and believes it may be a startup opportunity. She has searched online, watched videos, asked friends, and used AI tools, but she still does not understand the full process of validating, forming, building, and launching the company.

She may know isolated tasks such as “register an LLC,” “open a bank account,” or “build an MVP,” but she does not understand the dependencies or whether she is doing them too early.

## Goals

- determine whether the idea is worth pursuing;
- understand the complete startup journey;
- know the next best action;
- avoid unnecessary expenses;
- form the company correctly;
- complete legal and financial setup;
- reach launch without missing critical steps;
- feel confident that progress is real.

## Pain Points

- too many conflicting online recommendations;
- unclear legal and financial terminology;
- uncertainty about LLC versus C-Corp;
- repeated forms and information;
- fear of making an expensive mistake;
- difficulty comparing providers;
- no single place showing what is complete;
- feels overwhelmed by large checklists.

## Behaviors

- searches online before taking action;
- asks other founders for recommendations;
- uses free tools first;
- delays decisions when uncertain;
- prefers examples and plain language;
- wants confirmation before irreversible actions.

## Trust Needs

- clear explanation of why information is required;
- approval before filings, payments, or signatures;
- visible provider status;
- access to source documents and evidence;
- clear distinction between AI suggestions and verified facts;
- ability to request support.

## Product Expectations

StartupKit should:

- identify her current stage;
- show one focused next action;
- explain unfamiliar concepts;
- complete work where possible;
- reuse previously confirmed information;
- track provider work;
- store evidence;
- show the broader journey without overwhelming her.

## Representative Quote

> “I know I want to build this, but I do not know what I should do first or what I might be missing.”

## Success Definition

Aisha successfully moves from idea to validated plan, forms the company correctly, completes required setup, and reaches launch readiness with clear records and confidence.

# 4. Primary Persona — Technical Founder

## Profile

**Name:** Omar  
**Age range:** 24–40  
**Role:** Software engineer, technical lead, or technical founder  
**Startup stage:** MVP planning, building, or early launch  
**Team size:** One to five people  
**Technical knowledge:** High  
**Business experience:** Moderate or limited  
**Budget:** Will pay for speed and reliability  
**Decision style:** Fast when requirements are clear

## Background

Omar can build the product and understands cloud infrastructure, code, and software delivery. However, he has limited knowledge of company formation, founder equity, intellectual property, accounting, compliance, payroll, and startup operations.

He may postpone non-technical work until it becomes urgent.

## Goals

- establish an investor-ready company;
- protect founder and company IP;
- set up banking and accounting correctly;
- choose a practical technical stack;
- avoid administrative distraction;
- launch quickly;
- prepare for customers, hiring, and fundraising.

## Pain Points

- legal and finance work interrupts product development;
- uncertain which documents are required;
- providers use unfamiliar terminology;
- company records are scattered;
- no connection between technical and business milestones;
- repeat entry of founders, company details, and addresses;
- compliance work appears unexpectedly.

## Behaviors

- prefers automation and APIs;
- values clear system status;
- wants to inspect technical details when needed;
- dislikes unnecessary meetings;
- expects reliable integrations;
- may use multiple best-of-breed tools.

## Trust Needs

- provider-neutral architecture;
- reliable retries and status tracking;
- detailed audit history;
- versioned documents;
- clear security controls;
- no duplicate provider actions;
- ability to export data.

## Product Expectations

StartupKit should:

- coordinate legal and operational setup;
- integrate with technical tools;
- track dependencies;
- expose clear statuses;
- allow him to continue product work while StartupKit manages other processes;
- preserve an accurate Company Object.

## Representative Quote

> “I can build the product, but I do not want to spend weeks figuring out the legal and operational side.”

## Success Definition

Omar launches a functioning product with correct formation, founder documents, finance setup, technical infrastructure, and operational readiness.

# 5. Primary Persona — Non-Technical Founder

## Profile

**Name:** Sarah  
**Age range:** 25–45  
**Role:** Domain expert, operator, salesperson, or business founder  
**Startup stage:** Idea, validation, or MVP planning  
**Team size:** One to three people  
**Technical knowledge:** Low to moderate  
**Business experience:** Moderate  
**Budget:** Careful but willing to pay for execution  
**Decision style:** Outcome-focused

## Background

Sarah understands the customer and business problem but is unsure how to turn the idea into a product. She may depend on freelancers, agencies, no-code tools, or a future technical co-founder.

She needs help deciding what level of technical infrastructure is appropriate.

## Goals

- validate the customer problem;
- define a realistic MVP;
- avoid paying for unnecessary technology;
- create a website or product;
- establish brand and messaging;
- launch and reach customers;
- understand technical progress in plain language.

## Pain Points

- technical providers recommend different solutions;
- difficult to estimate cost and effort;
- uncertainty about no-code versus custom development;
- fear of being dependent on one agency;
- cannot easily judge whether technical work is complete;
- overwhelmed by cloud, hosting, database, and security terms.

## Behaviors

- focuses on customer and business outcomes;
- prefers guided decisions;
- values examples and recommendations;
- needs progress explained clearly;
- may outsource technical execution.

## Trust Needs

- clear scope and cost;
- visible deliverables;
- evidence of completed setup;
- plain-language explanations;
- ability to review before publication;
- controlled access for contractors.

## Product Expectations

StartupKit should:

- convert the idea into an MVP plan;
- recommend an appropriate technical path;
- coordinate website and infrastructure setup;
- track work performed by providers or specialists;
- show what is ready for launch.

## Representative Quote

> “I understand the customer, but I need help turning that into something real without wasting money.”

## Success Definition

Sarah moves from a validated idea to a clear MVP, public presence, working product path, and customer-launch plan.

# 6. Primary Persona — Existing-Company Founder

## Profile

**Name:** Daniel  
**Age range:** 28–50  
**Role:** Founder of an existing early-stage company  
**Startup stage:** Formed, building, launched, or early revenue  
**Team size:** Two to fifteen people  
**Technical knowledge:** Varies  
**Business experience:** Moderate  
**Budget:** Will pay to reduce risk and clean up gaps  
**Decision style:** Pragmatic and time-sensitive

## Background

Daniel already has a company, but the setup is fragmented. Some documents are in email, some are in a provider portal, and some tasks may never have been completed.

He may have changed lawyers, accountants, banks, or technical providers.

## Goals

- understand current company readiness;
- identify missing legal, finance, technical, HR, or operational work;
- avoid repeating completed tasks;
- centralize company information and evidence;
- prepare for customers, hiring, or fundraising;
- correct risky or outdated setup.

## Pain Points

- no complete inventory of company state;
- uncertain whether documents are current;
- providers do not share status;
- difficult to prove completion;
- missing ownership or IP records;
- previous setup may not match the company’s current stage.

## Behaviors

- wants a fast audit rather than basic education;
- uploads existing documents;
- prefers gap-based recommendations;
- may require migration from multiple tools;
- values progress over lengthy onboarding.

## Trust Needs

- original documents must be preserved;
- extracted information must require verification;
- StartupKit must not overwrite verified facts silently;
- completed work must be recognized;
- corrective work must explain risk and priority.

## Product Expectations

StartupKit should:

- accept existing company information;
- extract and verify facts;
- assess W1–W8;
- mark proven work complete;
- create a personalized gap-remediation plan;
- continue from the correct stage.

## Representative Quote

> “We already have a company. I need to know what is missing, not start from the beginning again.”

## Success Definition

Daniel receives an accurate company-state view, resolves critical gaps, and continues into the next operational or growth stage.

# 7. Secondary Persona — International Founder

## Profile

**Name:** Hamza  
**Age range:** 23–40  
**Role:** Founder located outside the United States  
**Startup stage:** Pre-formation or early operations  
**Team size:** One to five people  
**Technical knowledge:** Varies  
**Business experience:** Limited to moderate  
**Budget:** Sensitive to hidden costs  
**Decision style:** Cautious because of eligibility concerns

## Goals

- form a U.S. company;
- obtain an EIN;
- understand banking eligibility;
- use U.S. payments;
- remain compliant;
- build credibility with customers and investors.

## Pain Points

- provider eligibility differs;
- no U.S. address or tax history;
- banking and KYC uncertainty;
- time-zone and support limitations;
- confusing tax obligations;
- fear that one rejected provider will block the journey.

## Product Expectations

- eligibility checks;
- provider-specific requirements;
- clear waiting states;
- manual fallback;
- transparent limits;
- secure document handling.

## Representative Quote

> “I need one place to tell me what I can actually do as a non-U.S. founder and what is blocking me.”

# 8. Supporting Persona — Co-Founder

## Profile

A co-founder joins an existing workspace and participates in selected actions.

## Goals

- verify personal information;
- review ownership and vesting;
- approve important decisions;
- sign documents;
- understand assigned work.

## Pain Points

- unclear responsibility;
- too much access or too little context;
- missed invitations or signatures;
- disagreements about the latest document version.

## Product Expectations

- secure invitation;
- role-based access;
- clear assigned tasks;
- exact-version approvals;
- visible deadlines;
- shared audit history.

---

# 9. Supporting Persona — Specialist or Administrator

## Profile

A legal, finance, technical, HR, operations, or internal support specialist completes or reviews assigned work.

## Goals

- receive complete required context;
- access only authorized company data;
- request missing information;
- review and complete work;
- preserve evidence and audit history.

## Pain Points

- incomplete founder information;
- repeated communication outside the product;
- unclear assignment status;
- lack of template and version control;
- insufficient permission boundaries.

## Product Expectations

- controlled work queue;
- permission-based access;
- internal and founder-visible comments;
- service-level targets;
- evidence requirements;
- audit records.

---

# 10. Cross-Persona Needs

All personas need:

- clear status;
- understandable language;
- save and resume;
- reliable security;
- visible responsibility;
- consistent company information;
- document history;
- provider tracking;
- evidence of completion;
- predictable next actions.

# 12. Research Questions

The team should validate:

- Which persona has the strongest willingness to pay?
- Which startup stage creates the highest urgency?
- Which tasks founders want managed rather than guided?
- How much detail each persona wants in the interface?
- Which provider failures are most painful?
- Which actions require human review?
- What information founders consider too sensitive for AI?
- Whether existing-company founders value audit and remediation enough to pay.
- Whether international founders require a separate product path.

---

# 13. Persona Validation Criteria

A persona is considered validated when:

- at least five representative users have been interviewed;
- recurring goals and pain points are confirmed;
- current behavior is documented;
- willingness to pay is explored;
- trust and security concerns are understood;
- critical workflows are ranked;
- product assumptions are updated based on evidence.
